Graph of Life

An Artificial Life Algorithm

An attempt at finding Open Ended Evolution with graph theory, evolutionary game theory and neural networks. No optimization — just mutation, evolution and natural selection.

The whole script is on the right and runs on its own — python3 explain_minimal.py. The graph is a real run: seed 5 from iteration 129, chosen because every mechanic fires in it.

Layout
All-pairs, so the graph spreads globally. At 0 only the springs act and it clumps tightly. Spreads each node's edges evenly — 180° at degree 2, 120° at degree 3. How closely all-pairs repulsion is approximated. Lower is more exact and much slower; the cost falls with the cube of this. Raise it on very large graphs.

Presets

Lineage

Every genotype a run ever held, as a line: from the frame it first appeared in to the last one it was seen in, thickening with how many agents carried it, joined to the genotype it mutated from. Clades sit together, so a family is a band.

Colour is ancestry. A founder is given a colour at random and every mutation nudges its parent’s, so relatedness is visible directly — and a run that ends one colour is a run where one founder’s descendants took the world.

Flow modules

What a module is. A group of agents found by asking where the tokens actually go — not who is joined to whom, which is the graph, and not who is related to whom, which is the lineage. Only who sends tokens to whom. A module is a set of agents that a token wandering the flow tends to stay inside.

How they are found. By the map equation: describe a random walk on the flow as compactly as you can. If naming the groups and then naming positions inside them takes fewer bits than naming every position outright, the groups are carrying real structure. How much shorter is the compression below — 0% means the flow has no group structure at all, and the method is built so that flow without structure gives back one module rather than inventing several.

Following them. A module here and a module next iteration are the same one when enough of their members overlap. That lets a module keep its name while its agents are replaced one at a time, and lets it drift across the graph. Which makes turnover the number worth watching: a module that lives a long time while its members keep changing is a pattern being carried by different matter each iteration — a thing no definition based on who belongs to it could see.

Needs a run recorded with decisions, since that is where what crossed each link is written down.

Literature

The ideas this simulation is built on, and the ones it has to be measured against. The claim under test is a single sentence — this algorithm shows open-ended evolution — and most of what follows exists either to say what that would mean or to say why it probably is not happening.

These summaries are written from working knowledge, not from the papers open on the desk. Treat every year and every attribution as needing a check before it goes anywhere that matters. The what it means here notes are the part worth arguing with.

Open-ended evolution

Evolutionary activity statistics

Bedau & Packard 1992; Bedau, Snyder & Packard 1998

The first serious attempt to make “is this system still evolving?” a number rather than an impression. Give every component — a gene, a rule, a genotype — an activity counter that ticks up for every timestep it is present and in use. Sum those counters and you get the cumulative activity of the whole population; watch how new components accumulate activity over time and systems fall into three classes. Class 1 has bounded diversity and no new activity: it settles. Class 2 has unbounded new activity but bounded diversity: things keep changing, nothing accumulates. Class 3 has both unbounded — new things keep appearing and the stock of things keeps growing. Class 3 is what the biosphere does, and by Bedau's own measurement none of the artificial life systems of the day reached it.

The counters need components that persist long enough to accumulate anything. A brain id here names a genotype — a copy keeps its source's id, only a mutation makes a new one — which is exactly the identity an activity counter needs, and the reason that change was made. What has not been done is the measurement itself: nothing in this project has yet plotted activity against a neutral shadow, which is the only form in which the number means anything.

MODES, and the persistence filter

Dolson, Vostinar, Wiser & Ofria 2019

A toolkit that splits open-endedness into four separately measurable things: change (the population is not what it was), novelty (something never seen before is here), complexity (the most complex thing present is more complex than before), and ecology (how many distinct niches are occupied). The move that makes it work is the persistence filter: only count a component after it has survived some number of generations, so that mutational noise — variants that appear and die immediately — does not register as novelty.

This filter matters more here than in most systems, because every reproduction mutates. Without a filter the novelty count would essentially be the birth rate, which measures nothing. Any honest activity or novelty statistic for this simulation has to pass through a persistence threshold first, and the threshold has to be reported, because it is a free parameter that can manufacture the answer.

Novelty that expands the space

Banzhaf et al. 2016; Taylor et al. 2016; Packard et al. 2019

The sharpest distinction in the field, and the strongest argument against the claim this project is testing. Novelty comes in grades. Variation is a new point in a space that was always there. Innovation is a new way of using that space. A transition changes the space itself — new state variables, new degrees of freedom, rules that were not previously expressible. Banzhaf and colleagues put it as the difference between novelty within a fixed possibility space and novelty that enlarges it, and argue that only the second deserves the name open-ended.

Take this seriously and the claim is in trouble. The brain architecture here is fixed at configuration time: a set number of layers of a set width, evolved by copy and mutation with no operator that adds a neuron, a layer, or an input. The space of possible genotypes is a fixed-dimensional box, and every run is a walk inside it. Under the strict reading this is variation, permanently, and no amount of running changes that. The counter-argument this project is actually making is that the space that matters is not the genotype space but the space of multi-agent structures — castles — whose size is not bounded by the architecture. That argument is only as good as the evidence that such structures exist and grow, which is what the rest of the Research tab is for.

Ablation as method

Soros & Stanley 2014

Rather than define open-endedness, propose necessary conditions for it — in their case, things like a minimal criterion for reproduction, and freedom to accumulate. Then test them the way one tests any causal claim: remove one, rerun, and show the open-endedness goes away. The methodological point outlives the particular conditions. A property that survives every ablation was never caused by the mechanism you claimed.

This is the single most useful method available to this project, because every mechanism it needs is already a toggle in the simulation settings: messages on or off, message pre-pass on or off, revolutions on or off, mutation rate, brain kind. If module persistence or lineage depth or activity is really produced by non-transitivity, turning revolutions off should visibly cost it. No such ablation has been run yet. Every claim in this project is correlational until one is.

The comparison class

Ray 1991 (Tierra); Ofria & Wilke 2004 (Avida); Channon 2001 (Geb); Yaeger 1994 (Polyworld)

The systems any new one gets compared to. Tierra put self-replicating machine-code programs in a shared memory and got parasites, hyper-parasites and social cheats within hours — none of them designed in. Avida turned that into an instrument, with a rewarded task hierarchy, and produced the well-known result that a complex function can only evolve if the useless-looking intermediate steps are also rewarded. Geb and Polyworld pushed at ecology and embodiment instead.

The instructive difference is that all of them let the genome grow. A Tierra creature's program can get longer; an Avida organism's genome can gain instructions. That is precisely the space-expanding novelty above, and it is what this system does not have. Where this system is unusual by comparison is that the interaction graph is itself evolved — in Avida the spatial arrangement is a fixed grid, whereas here who can reach whom is a product of what the agents did. That is the axis along which this simulation could plausibly say something the older systems could not.

Evolutionary game theory on graphs

Spatial structure rescues cooperation

Nowak & May 1992

Play the prisoner's dilemma on a lattice, where each cell copies its most successful neighbour, and cooperation survives — not because anyone is nice, but because cooperators form clusters whose interiors trade only with each other, and a cluster can grow faster than defectors can eat its edge. In a well-mixed population the same game exterminates cooperation completely. Structure, on its own, changes the outcome of the game.

This is the founding reason to expect anything interesting from a graph-structured version of a zero-sum game. It also sets the shape of what to look for: not a cooperative strategy, but a cooperative region, whose boundary is where the loss happens and whose interior is protected. That is very close to what this project has been calling a castle.

Amplifiers and suppressors of selection

Lieberman, Hauert & Nowak 2005

Evolutionary graph theory proper. Put a Moran process on a graph and the graph's shape changes the fixation probability of an advantageous mutant. Some graphs are amplifiers, raising fixation probability above the well-mixed value — a star is a strong one, and certain constructions amplify arbitrarily. Others suppress, making selection nearly neutral. The same mutant, the same advantage, and the outcome is set by the wiring.

The result is usually read as: choose your graph and you have chosen your selection strength. Here nobody chooses it. The agents build the graph — reproduction wires a newborn into its parent's neighbourhood, and links that carry no tokens are pruned — so amplification is endogenous and time-varying. A structure that made its own neighbourhood into an amplifier for its own variants would be a real finding, and it is measurable: fixation rate inside a persistent flow module against the rate outside it.

b/c > k

Ohtsuki, Hauert, Lieberman & Nowak 2006

A rule of startling simplicity: on a graph where each node has about k neighbours, cooperation is favoured when the benefit-to-cost ratio of the cooperative act exceeds the average degree. Sparse graphs help cooperators, dense ones hurt them, and the threshold is just the degree. The intuition is that a cooperator's help is spread over its neighbours while its cost is its own, so fewer neighbours means a larger share of the benefit returns to relatives and clustered partners.

Mean degree in these runs falls from about 3.9 to about 3.2 over a few hundred iterations. If the b/c rule transfers at all, that is the graph moving in the direction that favours cooperation, and it is doing so on its own. The caveat is real: the rule is derived for a fixed regular graph and a donation game, and this is neither. It is a hypothesis about why the degree falls, not an explanation, and the honest test is whether cooperative structure appears after the fall rather than before.

Five rules for the evolution of cooperation

Nowak 2006

The catalogue: kin selection (help relatives), direct reciprocity (help those who helped you), indirect reciprocity (help those with a good reputation), network reciprocity (help those near you, in a structured population), and group selection (groups of cooperators outcompete groups of defectors). Each comes with its own threshold condition.

Two of the five are simply unavailable here, and that is a design fact rather than an oversight. Agents have no persistent identity visible to one another — there is no way to recognise the neighbour who helped you last iteration — so direct and indirect reciprocity have nothing to attach to. What is available is network reciprocity, which is the graph structure itself, and kin selection, since a newborn is placed beside its parent and so starts life among relatives. Any cooperation found here has to be explained by those two, or by something the catalogue does not list.

Adaptive networks

Gross & Blasius 2008

A review of systems where the dynamics on the network and the dynamics of the network run at comparable speed and feed back into each other. The recurring finding is that this coupling produces things neither dynamic gives alone: spontaneous separation into distinct classes of node, robust self-organised states, and phase transitions with no external parameter driving them.

This simulation is squarely an adaptive network — tokens move over links, and the links themselves are made and cut by how tokens moved. One asymmetry is worth naming, because it shapes everything: edges are only created by reproduction, and only destroyed by carrying nothing. There is no operator that lets two existing agents form a new link. The topology therefore erodes by default and can only be rebuilt through birth, which puts a hard constraint on what structures are reachable and is a prime candidate for the next mechanism to add.

The game being played

Colonel Blotto

Borel 1921; Roberson 2006

Two commanders split a fixed force across several battlefields without seeing each other's split; each field goes to whoever committed more there, and the winner is whoever takes the most fields. The game has no equilibrium in pure strategies — any fixed allocation is beaten by one that concedes where it is strong and overwhelms where it is weak — and Roberson gave the equilibrium in mixed strategies for the continuous case, where the marginal on each field is uniform up to a bound.

This is the game phase, with two differences that matter. Resources are not fixed across rounds — winning gets you tokens, which are the resource — so success compounds. And the battlefields are the neighbours, so the board itself changes as agents are born and die. The no-pure-equilibrium property is the engine of non-transitivity: because there is no allocation that beats everything, there is always in principle a strategy that beats the current best. Whether the population finds such cycles is a separate, empirical question — and the measurement so far says conquest 2-cycles run at 0.87–0.91× the rate a random-neighbour null produces, which is to say below chance.

The Red Queen

Van Valen 1973

From the observation that extinction probability appears roughly independent of how long a taxon has already survived: species do not get safer with age. Van Valen's explanation was that the environment of any species is mostly other species, each of which is also improving, so an absolute gain in fitness buys no relative gain. The zero-sum law it rests on — that adaptive gains in one lineage come at others' expense — is the mechanism, not an obstacle to it.

This paper retired an argument this project was making. The claim had been that conserved tokens make the system zero-sum and therefore closed to open-ended evolution (research/Research.md §3.1, now marked retracted). Van Valen's whole point is that sustained evolutionary change is driven by a zero-sum relation, not prevented by it. The conservation law here bounds the total token count and nothing else; it says nothing about the complexity of the arrangements those tokens can be in.

Current Individual Ancestral Opponents

Cliff & Miller 1995

How to see progress in a coevolving population, where the usual fitness plot is useless because the yardstick moves. Play the current population against ancestors from every earlier generation and plot the results as a matrix. Genuine progress gives a clean gradient — later beats earlier, everywhere. A cycle gives a banded pattern: a strategy that loses to its immediate ancestor but beats one from further back.

Directly runnable here, and not yet run. Checkpoints already store whole populations, so a late population can be replayed against a stored earlier one under the same graph. It is the cleanest available test for whether the non-transitivity that the Blotto game makes possible is actually being used — which matters, since the conquest-cycle count came out below chance.

What counts as an individual

The major transitions

Maynard Smith & Szathmáry 1995

The history of life read as a short list of events in which entities that could previously reproduce independently could afterwards only reproduce as part of a larger whole: genes into chromosomes, prokaryotes into eukaryotic cells, cells into multicellular organisms, organisms into colonies. Each transition is a new level of selection, and each needs the same problem solved — how the parts are stopped from defecting against the whole.

This is the template for what a castle would have to be, and it also rules out an easy definition. Defining a castle as a set of agents sharing a lineage would make relatedness near 1 by construction, and kin selection would then explain the cooperation trivially — the argument would be circular. Symbiosis is a transition too, and eukaryogenesis is the famous case of a transition between unrelated parties. A castle here is therefore defined on token flow and nothing else, with genealogy kept strictly as a separate measurement so it can be used as evidence rather than assumed.

The Price equation

Price 1970

An exact identity splitting the change in any population average into a selection term — the covariance between fitness and the trait — and a transmission term, the fitness-weighted average of how much offspring differ from parents. It assumes nothing about the system, and it nests: apply it within groups and between groups and the two levels of selection separate cleanly.

The nested form is the tool for asking whether a castle is a unit of selection at all. Partition into flow modules, decompose the change in some trait into a between-module and a within-module component, and a between-module term that is consistently the larger one is what group-level selection would look like. Everything needed for it exists — a partition, a per-agent trait, a fitness proxy in token gain — and it has not been computed.

Information-theoretic individuality

Krakauer, Bertschinger, Olbrich, Flack & Ay 2020

Stop asking what an individual is made of and ask what it propagates. An individual is a subset of the world that carries information about its own past into its own future in excess of what its environment supplies — a region of high self-predictive information. The definition comes in degrees rather than a yes or no, and it explicitly allows an individual whose material components are entirely replaced.

This is the principled version of what the flow modules approximate. The map equation finds groups a token-walk tends to stay inside, which is a proxy for the real quantity: a set whose own state predicts its own next state better than the surroundings do. The proxy is used because it is cheap and already implemented. The measured module structure — 41.5% compression, longest-lived module 48 frames, 23% membership turnover per frame — is exactly the shape this definition anticipates, a persistent pattern whose matter is exchanged. It is also, as things stand, uncalibrated: no null model has been run against it, and a number without a null is not yet evidence.

Structure defined negatively

Crutchfield & Hanson 1993; Beer 2004 (on gliders in the Game of Life)

Computational mechanics finds structure in a spatially extended system by first learning the regular background — the pattern the system falls into when nothing is happening — and then declaring structure to be everything that background fails to predict. Gliders and domain walls are what is left over after the regularity is subtracted. Beer's reading of the glider makes the related point that a persistent pattern is individuated by its behaviour, not by its cells.

The most transferable methodological idea in this list, and the one this project keeps rediscovering the hard way. Structure is what a background model does not predict — which means the background model has to be built first. Counting cycles gave thousands of them and looked like a finding, until a random-neighbour null showed the rate was below chance. The flow-module compression has not yet been through the same treatment.

The map equation

Rosvall & Bergstrom 2008

Find community structure by compression. Describe a random walk on the network with a two-level code: one codebook naming modules, and a separate codebook naming positions inside each module, so that names can be reused across modules the way street names are reused across cities. The description length is L = plogp(q) − 2∑plogp(qᵢ) − ∑plogp(pₐ) + ∑plogp(qᵢ+pᵢ), and the best partition is whichever minimises it. Because it describes flow rather than counting edges, it finds where things go rather than where links happen to be.

Implemented and running — this is the Flow modules view in this tab. Built directly on the token allocations, so it reads the choices agents made rather than the graph they sit in, which is what makes it a candidate detector for castles defined by flow. Compression of 0% means the flow has no group structure at all; the method is constructed so that structureless flow returns one module rather than inventing several, which tests/test_flowmodules.js checks against a ring.

Method

Neutral models

Kimura 1968; Harvey & Thompson 2005 (on drift in artificial evolution); the standard practice throughout ecology

Most of what looks like structure is what randomness produces anyway. The neutral theory's lasting contribution is less the claim that most substitutions are neutral than the discipline it forced: a selective explanation now has to beat an explicit drift model before it is accepted at all.

The lesson this project has had to learn twice. Conquest cycles looked abundant in the thousands and turned out to run at 0.87–0.91× a random-neighbour null — below chance, so the finding was the opposite of the one first written down. The flow-module numbers on the previous page have no null model behind them yet and should be read as measurements of the method rather than facts about the system. Written into research/Research.md as a standing rule, alongside the other one earned the same way: nothing measured on fewer than about thirty seeds gets reported as an effect.

The long-term evolution experiment

Lenski, from 1988 onward

Twelve populations of E. coli, propagated daily for tens of thousands of generations, with samples frozen along the way so any ancestor can be revived and competed against its own descendants. One population evolved the ability to use citrate aerobically — after about thirty-one thousand generations, and only because earlier, apparently unremarkable mutations had made it reachable.

A corrective on timescale, and a rebuke to impatience. The single most consequential innovation in the best-observed evolution experiment ever run took thirty-one thousand generations and depended on potentiating changes that were invisible when they happened. Runs here are hundreds of iterations. The frozen fossil record has a direct analogue in the checkpoint files, and the replay-the-ancestor design is the same one CIAO calls for — which is a second reason to build it.
