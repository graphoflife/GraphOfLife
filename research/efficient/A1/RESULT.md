# A1. Does behaviour keep moving?

**Answer: bounded reshuffling.** The populations of two kept worlds differ far more than two halves of one population (first token: 0.27 to 0.47 against a floor of 0.06), and no more after 25,000 iterations than after 2,500 (slopes of the first-token distance −0.06 to 0.03; of the child decision −0.30 to 0.05). The same holds for the child decision and for "the first token stays home" alone. The populations are no further from their random founders than from each other.

So behaviour neither stands still nor goes anywhere. It is replaced, inside bounds, in less than 2,500 iterations. This fits a population whose common ancestor lies 100 to 200 iterations back and whose mutants are nearly new brains (B1).

**Caveat.** Kept worlds are 2,500 iterations apart, so how fast the reshuffling is cannot be read here.
